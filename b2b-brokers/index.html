Script 3 - The 20 Percent

Contrarian / Insight: the statistic your industry will not say out loud

Central angle

Most businesses that go to market never sell, 70 to 80 percent of them, because owners treat selling as a decision instead of a 12 to 18 month preparation, and the prepared 20 percent are the only ones who get out.

Hook (0-10s)

70 to 80 percent of businesses that go to market never sell. Not sell for less. Never sell at all. If your exit plan is "I will list it when I am ready," you need to hear the rest of this.

Body (11-90s)

Most owners believe selling a business works like selling a house: decide you are ready, list it, wait for a buyer. That belief is exactly why most businesses never sell. The Exit Planning Institute's data puts the number at 70 to 80 percent. The listing sits for years, tire kickers waste your time, and too many owners end up closing the doors on a business that took a lifetime to build.

The businesses in the 20 percent look different. Their earnings are documented and verifiable, and they do not depend on the owner showing up every morning. Take one of our current listings, a tree service company in North Idaho: $433,505 in gross revenue, $271,335 in seller discretionary earnings, asking $495,000. A buyer can check every number. That does not happen by accident.

It takes 12 to 18 months of preparation before the listing ever goes live: valuing the business "as is," finding what would kill the deal, and fixing it while there is still time. It is important to plan ahead when thinking of selling your business, whether that is sooner rather than later. The 20 percent are not lucky. They are prepared.
